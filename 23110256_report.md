## Phase 2

The `sscanf` call here loads its format string from `0x402194`. My first mistake was lazy: I had the
`.rodata` dump open from phase 1, saw `25642025 64` = `%d %d` sitting at `0x4021a0`, and typed two
numbers. Boom. The two format strings *overlap* -- `0x402194` is the full `%d %d %d %d %d %d` and
`0x4021a0` is just its tail, reused by the later phases. The `cmp $0x6,%eax` right after the call
would have told me that immediately if I had read forward instead of guessing.The loop is general: `-0x4(%rbp)` is `i`, initialised to 1, and `cmpl $0x5,-0x4(%rbp)` / `jle` at the bottom runs it while `i <= 5`. Inside, `%edx` gets `a[i]` from `-0x20(%rbp,%rax,4)` and `%eax`
gets `a[i-1]`. The moment it clicked was `add %eax,%eax` followed by `add $0x1,%eax` -- I first read
the doubling as a square, but `a+a` is `2a`, so the constraint is just `a[i] == 2*a[i-1] + 1`.
Nothing constrains `a[0]`, so I started at 1: `1 3 7 15 31 63`.

## Phase 3

Two numbers this time (`0x4021a0`, `cmp $0x2,%eax`). The first gate is `cmp $0x6,%eax` followed by
`ja`, and I nearly missed that it is `ja` and not `jg` -- unsigned, so a negative first number wraps
to something huge and explodes. That pins the first number to `0 to 6`.
Then `mov 0x4021a8(,%rax,8),%rax` and `notrack jmp *%rax`: scale 8 on a pointer, an indirect jump,
so `0x4021a8` is a jump table of seven 8-byte targets. My dead end was reading the `objdump -s`
bytes as-is -- `16154000` looked like a nonsense address until I remembered little-endian and read
it backwards as `0x401516`, which is exactly the instruction right after the `jmp`. I dumped the
table properly with `x/7gx 0x4021a8` in gdb `401516, 401525, 401536, 401545, 401556, 401565,
401576`. Each target compares the second number against a different constant -- `7, 0xd6, 2, 0xfa,
0xb, 0xc8, 1`. I took case 0, so `0 7`.

## Phase 4

Same `%d %d`. I wasted a while assuming the first number landed in `-0xc(%rbp)` and the second in
`-0x10(%rbp)` the other way round; tracing the `lea`s before the call settled it -- `%rdx` (third
arg) is `-0xc`, `%rcx` (fourth) is `-0x10`, so first goes to `-0xc`, second to `-0x10`. The
`test %eax,%eax` / `jle` then `cmp $0x8,%eax` / `jle` pair bounds the *second* number to `1..8`.
The switch on the first number is where it got interesting. Case 3 adds `0x14` (20) and jumps out.
Case 2 adds 5 and jumps out. Case 1 adds 2 at `0x40160e` -- and then has **no jump**, so it falls
straight into the `addl $0x5` at `0x401612` and ends up at 7. That fall-through was the click; I had
written case 1 as "2" in my notes and the arithmetic refused to work.
The loop after it does `sum += 2*j` for `j = 0 .. second-1`, i.e. `n(n-1)`. The final `cmpl $0x28`
wants 40. With base 7 I need 33, with base 5 I need 35, neither is `n(n-1)`. With base 20 I need 20,
and `5*4 = 20`. So `3 5`.

## Phase 5

Short function, and the whole phase is one helper. After the usual `%d %d` check it loads the second
number into `%esi` and the first into `%edi`, calls `absdiff` at `0x40164e`, and requires the result
to be `0x11` = 17.
`absdiff` is the `cmov` example from lecture made real. It computes `%edx = %edi - %esi` and
`%eax = %esi - %edi` -- *both* subtractions, unconditionally, before any comparison -- then does
`cmp %esi,%edi` and `cmovg %edx,%eax`. My wrong turn was AT&T operand order: I read `cmp %esi,%edi`
as "esi > edi" and concluded the function returned a signed difference that could be negative, which
made the target 17 look like it had only one solution. Reading it correctly as `edi - esi`, the
`cmovg` keeps `edi - esi` when `edi > esi` and otherwise keeps `esi - edi`. That is `|a - b|`, with
no branch anywhere in the function. Any pair 17 apart works; I used `20 3`.
## Phase 6

Six numbers again (format back at `0x402194`, `cmp $0x6,%eax`). The per-element guard is a `js`
after `test %eax,%eax` and a `cmp $0x9,%eax` / `jle`, so every entry has to be `0..9`.
The body is two chained array reads: `mov 0x404060(,%rax,4),%eax`, then that result feeds
`mov 0x4040a0(,%rax,4),%eax`. Scale 4 means `int`, and `objdump -t bomb` handed me the names
outright -- `lookup1` at `0x404060` and `lookup2` at `0x4040a0`, both `0x28` bytes, so 10 ints each.
`objdump -s -j .data bomb` gave `lookup1 = 7 3 9 1 8 2 6 4 0 5` and `lookup2 = 4 4 7 2 9 0 3 8 5 1`.
My dead end was assuming a phase this late had to want a permutation, so I spent time trying to make
the six inputs distinct-and-covering before noticing there is no such check -- the loop only
accumulates `lookup2[lookup1[x]]` and the final `cmpl $0x1e` wants a sum of 30. I tabulated the
composed map by hand (`0->8, 1->2, 2->1, 3->4, 4->5, 5->7, 6->3, 7->9, 8->4, 9->0`) and picked six
indices summing to 30: `8+7+9+5+1+0`. Answer `0 5 7 4 2 9`.
